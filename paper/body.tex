\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Ensembles of machine-learned interatomic potentials are the standard tool for
deciding which structures to label next, and the standard way to validate a
cheap approximation of one is to check that it reproduces the exact ensemble.
That is the wrong target. We separate two questions that the literature
conflates: whether exact committee disagreement predicts reference-force error at
all (\emph{oracle quality}), and what a cheap screening gate costs relative to
whichever oracle one has chosen (\emph{preservation}). Preservation is
independent of oracle quality, which makes the second claim robust to the first
failing.
On the first question, exact committee disagreement ranks top-5\% force error at
AUROC $\fsFactorALo$--$\fsFactorAHi$ across six molecular systems and
$\fsMPtrajGlobal$ across \fsMPtrajN\ periodic crystals evaluated with a frozen
foundation-model trunk, against a random null of $\fsMPtrajRandom$. A
sum-over-coordinates (\emph{global}) statistic beats the max-component rule used
in prior work in $\fsGlobalWins$ of $\fsGlobalCells$ cells---but only at fixed
system size, because it is extensive; at variable size it acquires structures
$\fsMPtrajExtensivity\times$ larger than the intensive alternative and the
ordering reverses. On the second, a \emph{deterministic} leading-subspace
estimator skips $\fsLOSkipLo$--$\fsLOSkipHi$ of exact evaluations while
preserving the exact policy's decisions, and beats a randomised low-rank sketch
at matched cost on $\fsLOBeatsCV$ of $\fsLOSystems$ systems; an exact
finite-sample law for the randomised estimator predicts its measured failure to a
mean absolute error of $\fsBetaMAE$ with no free parameters.
The result practitioners should act on is the least flattering. On a real
materials pool the max mean-force norm---free, already computed in the forward
pass---reaches AUROC $\fsMPtrajFree$ against the committee's $\fsMPtrajGlobal$,
and adding the committee on top of it changes that by $\fsCompMarginal$ ---
not significant.
On fixed-composition systems the committee does add significantly, on
$\fsCompFixedSig$ of $\fsCompFixedN$ systems by $\fsCompFixedLo$--$\fsCompFixedHi$
AUROC. Committee uncertainty is therefore not redundant in general; it is
redundant where a free signal is already near-saturated, and every future claim
should be scored against that signal rather than against chance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) are trained on labels that cost
orders of magnitude more than the model does to evaluate, so which structures get
labelled matters more than almost any modelling choice. The dominant selection
heuristic is query-by-committee~\citep{seung1992query}: train an ensemble,
evaluate the spread of its predictions, and label where the spread is largest.
For force-field models the natural spread is over \emph{forces}, and obtaining it
exactly costs one reverse-mode pass per ensemble member.

A multi-head committee makes that cheaper in principle. Instead of $M$
independent models, one trunk carries $M$ output heads, so a single reverse pass
through the trunk with an appropriate head-space cotangent yields a projection of
the per-head force matrix. Whether a small number of such projections suffices to
reproduce the acquisition decisions of the exact committee is the question that a
sketching analysis is usually asked to answer.

We argue that this is the second question, and that asking it first has hidden a
weakness in the whole approach. The literature validates a cheap estimator
against the \emph{exact committee}, treating the committee as ground truth. But
the committee is itself a proxy---for the model's error against the reference
method---and it is that error a practitioner wants to find. So we decompose:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Factor A, oracle quality.} Does exact committee force disagreement
rank reference-force error? This is answerable offline from cached per-head
forces and requires no sketching at all.
\item \textbf{Factor B, preservation.} Given whichever oracle one has chosen, how
many of its decisions does a cheap gate reproduce, and at what cost?
\end{itemize}

Factor B is logically independent of Factor A: \emph{whatever you decided to
trust, we reproduce it more cheaply} is a claim that survives Factor A being
mediocre. Reporting them together, rather than reporting only the sketch fidelity,
is the paper's main methodological contribution, and it is what surfaces the
result in Section~\ref{sec:foundation}---that on a realistic materials pool the
committee loses to a signal that costs nothing.

Every numeric claim below is derived programmatically from a committed record;
none is transcribed. Section~\ref{sec:avail} describes the reproduction path.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Centred head space}
Let $F(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3N\times M}$ hold the forces predicted by $M$ committee
heads for a structure $x$ with $N$ atoms. Acquisition scores depend only on the
spread across heads, so the mean-force direction is separated out: with $Q_c$ an
$M\times r$ orthonormal (Helmert) basis of the centred subspace, $r=M-1$, the
quantity of interest is $A(x)=Q_c^{\top}F(x)^{\top}F(x)Q_c$, an $r\times r$
matrix independent of $N$. The per-coordinate variance is
$v_d=\lVert Q_c^{\top}F_d\rVert^2/(M-1)$.

\subsection{Three acquisition statistics}
Prior work ranks structures by the largest per-coordinate standard deviation,
$\max_d\sqrt{v_d}$ (\emph{max-component}). We also consider the largest per-atom
value (\emph{max-atom}) and the sum $\sum_d v_d$ (\emph{global}). The global
statistic is a trace of $A(x)$ and so is by far the easiest to estimate from few
projections; whether it is also the better physical predictor was pre-registered
as a secondary hypothesis before any of it was run.

\subsection{Estimators}
A projection (\emph{lane}) costs one reverse pass and returns $g_k=F w_k$ for a
head-space direction $w_k$. We compare Haar and Gaussian sketches, head
subsampling, a low-rank control variate that evaluates $r_0$ leading directions
exactly and sketches the residual, and a purely deterministic
\emph{leading-only} estimator that spends every lane on exact leading
eigendirections of the design-set Gram and discards the residual entirely.

\subsection{The calibrated gate}
The gate skips the exact computation when a cheap score $\hat{S}$ times a
calibration constant falls below a threshold. The constant is the
split-conformal one: $c_\alpha$ is the
$\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th order statistic of the calibration ratios
$S/\hat{S}$~\citep{vovk2005algorithmic,lei2018distribution}. When
$\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil > n$ no order statistic carries the finite-sample
guarantee and the implementation returns $+\infty$, which makes the gate skip
nothing---the safe failure. Realised coverage is verified against the
$\mathrm{Beta}(k,n{+}1{-}k)$ law by simulation rather than asserted.

Design, calibration and test roles are disjoint contiguous blocks with guard
bands sized from a measured integrated autocorrelation time; every split is
content-hashed and recomputes from its recorded seed. Confidence intervals use a
paired moving-block bootstrap~\citep{kunsch1989jackknife}.

\section{Does committee disagreement predict reference-force error?}
\label{sec:factora}

Across six molecular systems---3BPA~\citep{kovacs2021beyond} at 1200\,K under
three committee constructions, and three rMD17
molecules~\citep{christensen2020role}---exact global disagreement ranks the
top-5\% highest-error structures at AUROC $\fsFactorALo$--$\fsFactorAHi$, with a
random null at chance. The oracle is \emph{moderate}: useful, and far from
perfect. Nothing is fitted in this analysis, so there is no leakage risk.

\subsection{The global statistic wins---at fixed system size}
The global statistic beats max-component in $\fsGlobalWins$ of $\fsGlobalCells$
(system $\times$ error-score) cells, $\fsGlobalWinsSig$ of them significantly at
95\%, and in no cell is it significantly worse (Figure~\ref{fig:factora}). Because the global statistic is
also the one a sketch estimates most easily, the negative result about small
sketches (Section~\ref{sec:theory}) applies to the statistic one should not have
been using.

This holds \emph{at fixed $N$}, and the qualifier is load-bearing rather than
cosmetic; Section~\ref{sec:extensivity} shows the ordering reverse when system
size varies, and why it could not have shown up here.

\subsection{The margin over free signals}
Head-energy spread costs one forward pass; the norm of the mean force costs
nothing at all. Both are informative (AUROC 0.60--0.70). Exact global
disagreement beats the better of them by $\fsFreeGainLo$--$\fsFreeGainHi$ AUROC,
and pairwise the advantage is significant in $\fsFreeSig$ of $\fsFreeSigTotal$
comparisons. So the backward passes buy something real but not overwhelming---and
Section~\ref{sec:foundation} shows a regime where they buy nothing.

\section{What the cheap gate costs}
\label{sec:factorb}

Evaluated on held-out test blocks, the gate skips
$\fsLOSkipLo$--$\fsLOSkipHi$ of exact evaluations while reproducing the exact
policy's decisions. We report this count rather than a speedup because it is
independent of hardware, autodiff backend and batching.

\subsection{Randomisation does not earn its lanes}
At matched lane budget the deterministic leading-only estimator beats the
randomised control variate on $\fsLOBeatsCV$ of $\fsLOSystems$ systems, at every
budget tested, and has zero probe-seed variance by construction (Figure~\ref{fig:gate}). The mechanism is
specific to calibrated gates: $c_\alpha$ is fitted to the ratio $S/\hat{S}$, so a
systematic bias in $\hat{S}$ is absorbed exactly, and only its \emph{variance}
costs skip fraction. Leading-only is biased low by construction and pays nothing
for it. Since the head-space spectrum is concentrated---stable rank
$\approx 1.2$--$4.3$ of a possible $7$---a random projection is being asked to do
the one thing concentration makes hard.

The practical consequence is that the method gets simpler: at fixed budget, spend
every lane on exact leading directions.

\section{Theory: the negative result is predictable}
\label{sec:theory}

For a Haar sketch of $K$ of the $r$ centred directions, the ratio of estimated to
true per-coordinate variance is distributed exactly as
$\hat{v}_d/v_d \sim (r/K)\,\mathrm{Beta}(K/2,(r-K)/2)$. Injecting precisely this
noise into the exact variances and recomputing top-5\% recall reproduces the
measured recall across six systems and three budgets to a mean absolute error of
$\fsBetaMAE$, with no free parameters (Figure~\ref{fig:beta}).

Two corrections to the mechanism previously offered follow. First, a common
multiplicative bias cannot change a rank statistic, so the damage is estimator
\emph{variance}, not bias. Second, the coordinate directions cannot be treated as
isotropic: using the real head-space geometry roughly halves the prediction error
relative to an isotropic null, so coordinate coupling matters.

A Chernoff bound makes the result structural rather than incidental: uniform
componentwise accuracy at $\varepsilon=0.1$ requires $K$ of order
$10^3$--$10^4$, against an available budget of $K\le r=7$. The gap is two to
three orders of magnitude, so no amount of tuning recovers the max-component rule
from a small sketch.

\section{Generality}

\subsection{Committee size}
Enumerating \emph{all} $247$ subcommittees of every size across 20 systems,
halving the committee to $m=4$ retains $\fsMFourRetained$ of Factor A on average.
But the worst individual four-member subcommittee retains only
$\fsMFourWorstSubset$, and at $m=2$, $\fsMTwoWorstSubset$. Committee size buys
\emph{insurance}, not accuracy: going from four heads to eight moves the mean by
three points and the floor by twenty-seven.

\subsection{Independently trained committees}
Eight separately trained models, matched to shared-trunk counterparts on training
data and evaluation frames, confirm the global-statistic recommendation---so it is
a property of committees, not of the multi-head architecture. They are also far
better calibrated in magnitude: overconfidence $\fsNaiveOverconfLo\times$ against
$\fsSharedOverconfHi\times$ for the matched shared-trunk committee. The
\emph{acceleration}, by contrast, does not transfer at all, and we state this as a
derivation rather than a measurement: with no shared trunk, obtaining $Fw$
requires all $M$ columns of $F$, so no sketch can be cheaper than the exact
quantity. Of five pre-registered predictions in this experiment,
$\fsNaivePassed$ were confirmed.

\subsection{Condensed phase and distribution shift}
Bulk liquid water ($N=192$, periodic, $D=576$) and a same-temperature
MD-versus-PIMD pair give a distribution shift in which only quantum nuclear
effects differ. One caveat must survive contact with the reader: the shift
regime's striking Factor A is substantially a symptom of \emph{this model family
failing badly there}. A matched independently-trained committee is $2.7\times$
more accurate on the shifted data and its Factor A collapses, while a free signal
retains most of the apparent skill. Shift-regime claims must therefore be quoted
beside the free-signal baseline and the accuracy gap.

\section{Foundation models, real pools, and the baseline that must be beaten}
\label{sec:foundation}

The setting the method is actually pitched at is a foundation model with
committee readouts on a frozen trunk~\citep{batatia2023foundation}. We scan
\fsMPtrajN\ periodic crystals drawn from MPtraj~\citep{deng2023chgnet},
spanning 89 elements and 1--444 atoms, with a MACE-MP trunk fine-tuned to carry
eight disjoint committee heads. Five predictions were pre-registered; two were
confirmed.

\subsection{Factor A transfers}
Exact committee disagreement reaches AUROC $\fsMPtrajGlobal$ here against a
random null of $\fsMPtrajRandom$. Committee force uncertainty is not an artefact
of small organic molecules.

\subsection{Extensivity, and why the global statistic reverses}
\label{sec:extensivity}
The global statistic sums $v_d$ over $3N$ coordinates, so it grows with system
size whether or not the model is uncertain. Acquiring by it returns structures
whose median atom count is $\fsMPtrajExtensivity\times$ that of the
max-component selection, and the ordering of Section~\ref{sec:factora} reverses
on all four error scores (Figure~\ref{fig:extensivity}a, Table~\ref{tab:mptraj}). Every system in Section~\ref{sec:factora} has fixed
$N$, where an extensive and an intensive statistic differ by one constant and the
pathology \emph{cannot} appear: the recommendation was never wrong where it was
measured, it was measured only where it could not fail.

The repair is a size normalisation, $S_\alpha=(\sum_d v_d)/(3N)^\alpha$, with
$\alpha$ chosen on a design split and every reported number taken from the
disjoint remainder. The selected $\alpha=\fsAlphaStar$ raises held-out AUROC from
$\fsNormAurocZero$ to $\fsNormAurocStar$ (Figure~\ref{fig:extensivity}b), and at fixed $N$ it changes nothing
exactly---so it can be adopted globally at no cost. We report the outcome
precisely: across four error scores the normalised statistic achieves
$\fsNormWins$ significant wins, one tie, and $\fsNormLosses$ significant loss
against max-component. This restores \emph{parity}, not superiority, and
$\alpha$ is pool- and target-dependent rather than universal.

\subsection{The free signal wins here, and is not complemented}
The max mean-force norm reaches AUROC $\fsMPtrajFree$ against the committee's
$\fsMPtrajGlobal$---a signal that is already computed in the forward pass
outranking eight backward passes (Table~\ref{tab:mptraj}). The advantage survives stratification by atom
count and, within force-magnitude quintiles, the committee still beats chance,
so it does carry information beyond force magnitude; a free proxy is simply at
least as good.

Because the free signal is free, the decision a practitioner faces is not
``committee or free signal'' but ``free signal, or free signal \emph{plus} eight
backward passes''. Nested models fitted on a design split and scored on held-out
data give AUROC $\fsCompFree$ for the free signal alone and $\fsCompBoth$ with the
committee added---a marginal of $\fsCompMarginal$, not significant. On
fixed-composition systems the same comparison gives significant gains on
$\fsCompFixedSig$ of $\fsCompFixedN$ systems, spanning
$\fsCompFixedLo$--$\fsCompFixedHi$. The committee is redundant \emph{here}, not
in general, and the discriminating variable is how close the free signal already
is to saturation.

\subsection{The selection-overlap bound is unavailable}
One can hope to bound the downstream active-learning difference without
retraining, by showing the gate acquires nearly the same set as the exact
statistic. It does not: Jaccard overlap is $\fsMPtrajJaccard$ at a realistic
acquisition rate, short of the pre-registered threshold. Overlap statistics must
always be reported with their acquisition rate, since any two rankings agree when
a large fraction of the pool is taken.

\section{Wall clock}

Lane reduction pays only once the backward pass is compute-bound rather than
launch-bound, and the controlling variable is the total edge count in the batch,
not the number of structures. Measuring both systems in a single exclusive job at
identical settings, water reaches $\fsWaterSpeedupBOne\times$ at $B=1$ rising to
$\fsWaterSpeedupBSixteen\times$ at $B=16$ on an A100,
$\fsWaterSpeedupBSixteenHTwo\times$ on an H200 and
$\fsWaterSpeedupBSixteenBlk\times$ on an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell---three GPU
generations agreeing to $\fsWaterSpeedupSpread$\%---against
$\fsThreeBPASpeedupBSixteen\times$ for 3BPA in the same job (Figure~\ref{fig:speedup}, Table~\ref{tab:pairs}). Because the
threshold is a property of the machine as well as the system, the batch size at
which lane reduction begins to pay \emph{increases} with how fast the GPU is.

Acceleration is deliberately not the headline. The count of avoided exact
evaluations is hardware-independent and survives every timing outcome.

\section{Recommendations and limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Score committee uncertainty against the free mean-force norm}, not
against a random null. On some system classes it wins by a wide margin; on others
it adds nothing measurable.
\item \textbf{Use the global statistic at fixed system size}; normalise by
$(3N)^{\alpha}$ for variable size and refit $\alpha$ per pool and per target.
\item \textbf{Prefer a deterministic leading subspace to a randomised sketch}, at
equal budget.
\item \textbf{Committee size buys insurance.} To fix magnitude calibration,
change the construction, not $M$.
\end{enumerate}

Limitations. One architecture family; $M=8$ with subcommittee interpolation and
no trained $M=16$ control. No retraining was performed, so no claim is made about
downstream model quality---only about which structures are selected. The
selection-overlap bound is unavailable, so that gap cannot be closed cheaply.
$\alpha$ is pool-dependent. Finally, several results here correct earlier
statements of our own; the record of those corrections is retained in the
repository rather than silently overwritten.

\section*{Data and Software Availability}
\label{sec:avail}
All statistical results regenerate on CPU from repository-tracked artifacts:
\texttt{tools/reproduce.py --tier offline} re-derives every headline quantity in
roughly four minutes with no model execution, no CUDA and no download, and checks
each against its committed value. \texttt{tools/audit.py} verifies the frozen
prior-work tree, the sha256 binding of every pre-registered protocol to a
registration record written before that experiment produced any artifact, the
provenance of every number in this manuscript, the split manifests, and the

verdict as data, alongside the confidence stated for it in advance.
