# J1 — statistical design repair

Every number generated by `experiments/j1_build_splits.py` and the CI scripts;
manifests in `manifests/splits/`, summary in `results/records/j1_splits.json`.

## What was wrong

The frozen tree has **two divergent split implementations** —
`screening/fallback_gate.py:169` (`int(round(0.2n))`) and the inline one at
`scripts/07_gate_baselines.py:202` (`int(0.2*S)`) — which disagree by a structure
for most S and produce different index sets. Neither writes a manifest, so
nothing downstream can state which was used. Both are now permanently banned from
journal code by an AST-based test (`test_banned_frozen_helpers_are_never_imported_or_called`),
and neither frozen file is edited.

Both also split MD trajectory frames with `torch.randperm`, with no temporal
blocking — the objection a referee raises first.

## Frame indices recovered

The offline track's only external dependency. rMD17 cache row `i` corresponds to
trajectory frame `test_ids[i]`; `test.csv` was fetched from Zenodo by HTTP Range
(4886 B) and **verified against the central directory's CRC-32** — a check
`utils/fetch.py:119` currently omits, since it validates only the inflated
length. `test.csv` is **sorted ascending** (min 9, max 9994, median stride 7), so
cache row order *is* trajectory time order and contiguous blocking on the row
index is genuine temporal blocking. 3BPA rows follow `test_1200K.xyz` file order.

## The leakage question, measured rather than assumed

Integrated autocorrelation time along the trajectory ordering, by Sokal
automatic windowing (estimator validated against analytic AR(1): recovers
τ = 1.00/1.85/3.94/9.13/18.50 against 1.00/1.86/4.00/9.00/19.00):

| system | ρ(1) | τ_int | guard |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3BPA disjoint | 0.165 | 3.96 | 8 |
| 3BPA same | 0.067 | 1.51 | 4 |
| 3BPA overlapping | 0.016 | 1.06 | 3 |
| rMD17 ethanol / aspirin / azobenzene | ≈0 | 1.00–1.18 | 2–3 |

**Autocorrelation is weak everywhere.** rMD17's `test.csv` subsamples with median
stride 7 from a 10,000-frame run, which has already decorrelated the frames; 3BPA
at 1200 K decorrelates fast on its own. Comparing Factor A on each scheme's test
role:

| system | blocked test | random test | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3BPA disjoint | 0.767 | 0.806 | -0.038 |
| 3BPA overlapping | 0.784 | 0.765 | +0.019 |
| 3BPA same | 0.847 | 0.867 | -0.020 |
| rMD17 ethanol | 0.760 | 0.707 | +0.053 |
| rMD17 aspirin | 0.742 | 0.744 | -0.002 |
| rMD17 azobenzene | 0.836 | 0.807 | +0.029 |

Mean |difference| = **0.027**, 3 positive and
3 negative; the largest single difference is 0.053.

> **Regenerated after a cross-document audit.** The table previously here was
> computed from the splits as they stood BEFORE the seed was mixed with the
> system name, and was never recomputed when the manifests were — in the same
> document that describes the regeneration. It gave mean |difference| 0.017; the
> shipped manifests give 0.027. It is now produced by
> `experiments/j1_split_comparison.py` into
> `results/records/j1_split_comparison.json`.
>
> **The conclusion survives, with a thinner margin that has to be stated.** The
> differences are larger than first reported and ethanol's (+0.053)
> exceeds the ~0.04 bootstrap half-width the original argument leaned on. But
> leakage has a *signature*: a random-frame split that leaked would inflate the
> random-split score consistently. The signs remain mixed
> (3/3), and the largest single difference favours
> the **blocked** split — the opposite of a leakage pattern. So the defensible
> claim is now "no consistent leakage signature, with per-system differences up to
> 0.05", not "the difference is sampling noise".

**So the workshop's random-frame split was not materially leaky, and we can now
prove it rather than hope it.** The blocked scheme is still adopted as primary —
it is the defensible default and costs 16–96 dropped frames — but the honest
headline is that the objection does not bite on these systems. That is a much
stronger position than either ignoring the issue or silently switching schemes.

every role samples the entire trajectory (otherwise a slow drift would be
perfectly confounded with the role), guard bands dropped only at boundaries where

## Calibration power — correcting an assumption in the plan

Realised split-conformal coverage is `Beta(k, n_cal+1-k)` with
`k = ceil((n_cal+1)(1-α))`:

| system | n_cal | α=0.10 | α=0.05 | α=0.01 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3BPA (all three) | 395–416 | 0.902 [0.876, 0.925] | 0.952 [0.933, 0.968] | 0.992 [0.984, 0.998] |
| rMD17 (each) | 188–196 | 0.905 [0.867, 0.937] | 0.952 [0.925, 0.975] | 0.995 [0.984, 1.000] |

The plan asserted α=0.01 would be "feasible but not reportable" on rMD17. **That
was too pessimistic and is corrected here.** With n_cal ≈ 194 (not the 200 the
plan assumed from a 20% share of 1000, before guard-band drops), `k = n_cal`, so
`c_α` is the *maximum* observed ratio. The consequence is not under-coverage —
Beta(194,1) gives mean 0.995 and a 5th percentile of 0.985, i.e. it
*over*-covers, which is conservative and safe. The real cost is that a maximum
order statistic is sensitive to a single outlying ratio, so the **skip fraction**
becomes high-variance while coverage stays safe. That is the accurate
characterisation: report α=0.01, but report its skip fraction with an interval
and note the mechanism.

> **Superseded once water was added.** Water's blocked splits give n_cal = 84–94,
> and `alpha_feasible(n_cal, 0.01)` is **False** there — `ceil((n_cal+1)·0.99) >
> n_cal`, so no order statistic carries the guarantee and `conformal_c` correctly
> returns +inf. So α=0.01 is reportable on 3BPA and rMD17 but **infeasible on
> water**, and the recommendation above must be read as system-dependent. The
> feasibility flag is already persisted per system in `results/records/j1_splits.json`.

## Machinery built and validated

- `evaluation/autocorr.py` — Sokal-windowed τ_int, validated against analytic AR(1).
- `evaluation/bootstrap_block.py` — paired IID and moving-block bootstrap.
  **Validated by coverage simulation**: on AR(1) with τ_int = 4, the IID bootstrap
  covers the true mean only 62.3% of the time at nominal 95%; the block bootstrap
  restores 89.0%. That is the concrete demonstration that blocking matters when
  autocorrelation is present — and, together with the measured τ_int ≈ 1 here, why
  it happens not to matter for these particular caches.
- `evaluation/tail_metrics.py` — AUROC/AUPRC/recall/enrichment/risk-coverage,
  fully vectorised over a leading resample axis (a 10,000 × 2,139 AUROC takes
  3.6 s). Verified against brute-force all-pairs AUROC in float64, tie-averaged
  ranks, a hand-computed average-precision example, and a per-row loop.
- `data/splits.py` — the single split implementation, manifests, content hashing,
  `alpha_feasible`, and the Beta coverage law.
- `tests/test_splits.py` — 20 tests, all passing, including guard-band temporal
  separation and the **NaN-poisoning proof** that `leading_head_directions` reads
  design rows and nothing else (poison every non-design structure with NaN; the
  basis must come back finite and bit-identical).
