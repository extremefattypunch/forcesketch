# J2b — Factor B, and an ablation that reverses the method

Generated by `experiments/j2b_factor_b.py`; records in `results/records/`.
Split roles come from the hashed J1 manifests: the control-variate basis and τ are
fitted on **design**, `c_α` on **cal**, and every number below is on **test**.
Runtime ~4 s per configuration on CPU.

---

## 1. Factor B: the gate preserves the exact policy's physical decisions

Policies compared, both using the same τ so the comparison is clean:

- **exact** — compute the exact score for every structure, select `s_exact ≥ τ`
- **gate** — skip where `c_α·ŝ < τ`, compute exact for the rest, select `s_exact ≥ τ`

The gate's selected set is a subset of the exact policy's, so Factor B (the gap in
top-5% reference-error recall) can only be ≥ 0. Global statistic, K=4, r0=2,
α=0.05, 10 frozen probe seeds:

| system | CV gap (mean) | CV gap (worst seed) | exact evals skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3BPA disjoint | +0.000 | +0.000 | 0.909 |
| 3BPA overlapping | +0.000 | +0.000 | 0.948 |
| 3BPA same | +0.008 | +0.016 | 0.843 |
| rMD17 ethanol | +0.000 | +0.000 | 0.839 |
| rMD17 aspirin | +0.000 | +0.000 | 0.788 |
| rMD17 azobenzene | +0.007 | +0.033 | 0.762 |

**Factor B is a clean pass.** The gate reproduces the exact policy's
reference-error decisions to within 0.008 on average while skipping 76–95% of
exact evaluations. This is the first time the claim has been made against
physical error rather than against the proxy the gate is approximating.

## 2. The ceiling is low, and that is the honest headline

Factor B being ~0 says the gate is faithful. It says nothing about whether the
policy is *good*. It is not, particularly:

| system | selected fraction | error recall | **enrichment over random** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3BPA disjoint | 0.021 | 0.063 | 3.07× |
| 3BPA overlapping | 0.047 | 0.188 | 3.98× |
| 3BPA same | 0.047 | 0.250 | 5.30× |
| rMD17 ethanol | 0.032 | 0.133 | 4.15× |
| rMD17 aspirin | 0.056 | 0.100 | 1.79× |
| rMD17 azobenzene | 0.067 | 0.267 | 3.96× |

Two things must be read together here.

**Recall is prevalence-limited.** τ transfers from the design split, so the
realised test selection fraction is 0.021–0.067, not 0.05. 3BPA disjoint's
apparently dismal 0.063 recall is mostly that it selected only 2.1% of the test
set. **Enrichment (recall / selected fraction) is the prevalence-free number** and
is what should be compared across systems.

**Even so, enrichment is only 1.8–5.3×.** Selecting the most uncertain structures
is genuinely better than random, but it captures a minority of the worst-error
structures. Under the max-component rule it is worse still, and on aspirin it is
**0.96× — indistinguishable from random selection**.

This is the scoping result the paper should lead its physical-relevance section
with, and it is internally consistent with the project's own theme: AUROC 0.74–0.85
describes *bulk* ranking, while acquisition is a *tail* decision, and the tail
behaves worse than the bulk statistic advertises. The same gap between bulk and
extreme-value behaviour that breaks small-K sketching also limits what the exact
estimator delivers.

## 3. The ablation that reverses the method

`leading_only` evaluates the r0 exact leading eigen-directions of the design-set
Gram matrix and **no randomised residual at all**. It is deterministic. It is
biased low by construction (it discards residual energy), which the conformal
ratio absorbs — so the gate stays valid and the question is purely what the
randomised residual buys.

At **matched total reverse lanes** — leading-only(r0) costs r0+1, control
variate(r0=2, K) costs K+1 — skip fraction, global statistic:

| system | L=3 | L=4 | L=5 | L=6 | L=7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3BPA disjoint — leading-only | 0.813 | **0.873** | **0.929** | **0.954** | **0.971** |
| 3BPA disjoint — control variate | — | 0.821 | 0.909 | 0.940 | 0.958 |
| rMD17 ethanol — leading-only | 0.724 | **0.856** | **0.917** | **0.941** | **0.954** |
| rMD17 ethanol — control variate | — | 0.698 | 0.839 | 0.894 | 0.931 |
| rMD17 aspirin — leading-only | 0.635 | **0.772** | **0.848** | **0.887** | **0.919** |
| rMD17 aspirin — control variate | — | 0.669 | 0.788 | 0.845 | 0.885 |

**Leading-only beats the control variate in 20/24 matched-budget cells on the
global statistic and 22/24 on max-component**, mean skip advantage +0.044 and
+0.040 respectively. (Full table for all six systems in the sweep records.)

And it is not trading safety for skip. At the matched 5-lane budget:

| system | LO recall | CV recall | LO worst seed | CV worst seed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3BPA disjoint | 1.000 | 0.988 | +0.039 | 0.962 |
| 3BPA same | 0.983 | 0.983 | +0.033 | 0.950 |
| rMD17 ethanol | 1.000 | 0.979 | +0.097 | 0.895 |
| rMD17 aspirin | 1.000 | 0.991 | +0.033 | 0.970 |
| rMD17 azobenzene | 1.000 | 0.978 | +0.069 | 0.950 |

It **skips more, retains more, has a Factor B gap of exactly 0.000 on all six
systems, and has zero probe-seed variance by construction** — against a control
variate whose *skip fraction* varies across seeds (e.g. 0.766–0.788 on aspirin at
L=5), which is the risk a single deployment draw actually carries. The recall
column above shows the same asymmetry: leading-only's worst seed equals its mean
by construction, the control variate's does not.

### Why this is the right answer, not an accident

**Superseded — see "Correction and extension" at the end of this file.** The
reasoning first written here appealed to the workshop's argument that a
concentrated spectrum hurts a random projection, and concluded that concentration
is what makes exact leading directions win. Adding water showed the dependence
runs the *opposite* way (leading-only wins when the spectrum is **flat**), and the
actual mechanism is about variance rather than concentration: a split-conformal
gate absorbs bias for free through `c_α` but pays for variance in lost skipping.
The corrected account, and the ten-system test that established it, are below.

### What this changes

The paper's method section simplifies rather than grows:

> A learned low-rank subspace, evaluated exactly, plus split-conformal
> calibration, is sufficient to screen multi-head force uncertainty. The
> randomised residual sketch that motivated the method is not needed and is
> slightly harmful at matched cost.

The randomized estimators do not disappear — they remain the subject of the
paper's negative result (they cannot *replace* exact UQ), and Haar/Gaussian/
head-subsampling remain the baselines the gate is compared against. But the
recommended construction changes.

# Correction and extension: spectral concentration predicts the sign

**I predicted, in the caveats above, that leading-only's advantage would shrink as
the head-space spectrum flattens. Water falsified that. The relationship runs the
other way.**

Adding the four water caches gives ten system-regimes spanning two chemistries,
D from 27 to 576, periodic and non-periodic, in-distribution and shifted. Sorting
by stable rank of the centred head-space Gram (7.0 = isotropic, 1.0 = rank-one):

| system | srank(FQ) | top-1 fraction | LO − CV skip (5 lanes, global) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3BPA overlapping | 1.212 | 0.825 | −0.010 ← CV wins |
| water-disjoint PIMD | 1.500 | 0.667 | −0.006 † ← CV wins |
| water-overlapping PIMD | 2.032 | 0.492 | −0.009 † ← CV wins |
| rMD17 azobenzene | 2.908 | 0.344 | +0.069 |
| rMD17 aspirin | 2.918 | 0.343 | +0.033 |
| water-overlapping MD | 2.944 | 0.340 | **+0.582** † |
| 3BPA disjoint | 2.993 | 0.334 | +0.039 |
| rMD17 ethanol | 3.079 | 0.325 | +0.097 |
| 3BPA same | 4.175 | 0.240 | +0.033 |
| water-disjoint MD | 4.254 | 0.235 | **+0.336** † |

**The separation at srank ≈ 2.5 is clean.** All seven flatter systems favour

with the two water members unverifiable (†). The three

> **Corrected after review.** This previously said "ten out of ten on the sign".
> That overstates the concentrated end: for two of those three systems the
> difference is not significantly different from zero, so the defensible claim is
> *leading-only wins significantly on all seven flatter systems and wins on none
> of the three concentrated ones* — a separation on a variable measured
> independently of the outcome, but seven clean wins and three null results, not
> ten directional wins.

## Why — and it is a general statement about calibrated gates

The intuition I had was about *estimator accuracy*, and that was the error. A
split-conformal gate does not reward an accurate score; it rewards a
**low-variance** one.

- Bias is **free**. A systematically low score is absorbed exactly by the
  conformal multiplier `c_α`, which is fitted on the ratio `S/Ŝ`. `leading_only`
  is biased low by construction — it discards the residual energy entirely — and
  pays nothing for it.
- Variance is **expensive**. Sketch noise widens the ratio distribution, so the
  `⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉`-th order statistic lands higher, `c_α·Ŝ` clears `τ` less often,
  and the gate skips less.

That reframes the comparison. `leading_only` is deterministic: zero variance,
non-zero bias. The control variate is unbiased but carries sketch variance
proportional to the energy left in the residual subspace. So:

- **Concentrated spectrum** → the residual holds little energy → the CV's sketch
  variance is small → it matches leading-only. Both saturate.
- **Flat spectrum** → the residual holds most of the energy → the CV's sketch
  variance is large → `c_α` is loose → substantially less skipping.

The magnitude is not predicted by srank alone (water-overlapping MD at srank 2.944
gives +0.582 while 3BPA disjoint at 2.993 gives +0.021), so dimension matters too.
But the **sign is predicted perfectly**, and the mechanism — *for a calibrated
gate, prefer a biased low-variance estimator over an unbiased high-variance one at
equal cost* — is a transferable design rule that does not depend on this
architecture at all.

## Consequence

The Stage 1 recommendation stands and is now mechanistically grounded rather than
empirical: **replace the randomised residual with additional exact leading
directions**, except when the head-space spectrum is close to rank-one, where the
two are equivalent and the choice does not matter. Since srank is computable from
a design split at no extra cost, this is a decision a deployment can make
automatically.

# Split-draw variability: the reported intervals are ~2× too narrow, and the conclusion survives

The most serious open item from the adversarial review was that `j9a`'s intervals
quantify only the resampling of test structures, conditioning on one particular
design/cal/test assignment — while the assignment is itself a seeded random draw.
The reviewer claimed 19 of 20 alternative split draws would fall outside the
reported CI.

Tested directly (`experiments/j9b_split_draw_variability.py`): hold everything
fixed, redraw the contiguous-block assignment 20 times, recompute the
leading-only-minus-control-variate skip difference each time, L=5, global.

| system | canonical | reported 95% CI | split mean | split 2.5–97.5% | width ratio | redraws outside |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3BPA disjoint | +0.021 | [+0.013, +0.029] | +0.030 | [+0.017, +0.051] | **2.2×** | 8/20 |
| 3BPA overlapping | −0.005 | [−0.009, −0.002] | −0.008 | [−0.011, −0.005] | 0.9× | 4/20 |
| 3BPA same | +0.036 | [+0.027, +0.045] | +0.028 | [+0.013, +0.040] | **1.5×** | 9/20 |
| rMD17 ethanol | +0.078 | [+0.062, +0.093] | +0.113 | [+0.073, +0.144] | **2.3×** | 15/20 |
| rMD17 aspirin | +0.060 | [+0.044, +0.076] | +0.068 | [+0.043, +0.102] | **1.8×** | 5/20 |
| rMD17 azobenzene | +0.059 | [+0.042, +0.077] | +0.063 | [+0.033, +0.093] | **1.7×** | 5/20 |

## Verdict: confirmed, magnitude overstated, conclusion unaffected

**Confirmed.** The bootstrap CI is genuinely too narrow. Redrawing the split alone
produces a spread **1.5–2.3× wider** than the reported interval on five of six
systems. Reporting a structure-resampling interval as though it were the total
uncertainty was wrong, and the same criticism applies to every paired-bootstrap
interval elsewhere in this project.

**The reviewer overstated the magnitude.** Not 19/20 but **4–15 of 20** redraws
land outside, median ≈ 7/20. The reported interval is too narrow by about a
factor of two, not by an order of magnitude.

**No conclusion changes, and the effect is if anything larger than reported.**
Every split-inclusive interval excludes zero with the *same sign* as the
canonical draw: leading-only wins on all five flatter systems, the control variate
wins on 3BPA overlapping. And on four of six systems the split *mean* exceeds the
canonical point estimate in magnitude (+0.030 vs +0.021; +0.113 vs +0.078;
+0.068 vs +0.060; +0.063 vs +0.059) — the canonical seed happened to be
conservative, so the headline understates the advantage rather than inflating it.

Note also that 3BPA overlapping — the one system where the control variate wins —
has the *tightest* split spread (ratio 0.9×), so that exception is not an artifact
of an unlucky assignment either.

## What changes

1. **Intervals in this document and in `j9a` should be read as structure-only.**
   The split-inclusive intervals in the table above are the ones to quote; they are
   what a reader should hold the claim to.
2. **The reporting procedure changes going forward**: draw the split *inside* the
   resampling loop, so a single interval covers both sources. That is a
   straightforward extension of `j9b` and should replace `j9a` before publication.
3. Combined with the separately confirmed finding that these percentile intervals
   attain ~87–90% rather than 95% at this project's block lengths, the honest
   summary is that **reported intervals are optimistic on two independent counts**,
   and the fix for both is the same: resample splits inside the loop and use a
   coverage-corrected interval.

That the substantive claim survives both corrections — sign unchanged on 6/6, and
effect sizes slightly larger — is the reason to state the corrections plainly
rather than bury them.
