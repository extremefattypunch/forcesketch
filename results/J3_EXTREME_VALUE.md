# J3-lite — the negative result, predicted rather than measured

Generated by `experiments/j3_extreme_value.py` (14 s, CPU);
records in `results/records/j3_extreme_value.json`.

The workshop paper attributes the top-5% recall failure to extreme-value *bias*
in `max_d σ̂_d`. That cannot be the whole story — recall is a rank statistic, and a
common multiplicative bias leaves every rank unchanged. This is the corrected
mechanism, and it turns out to be predictive.

---

## 1. The distributional law, verified

For a Haar sketch of K directions inside the r-dimensional centred head space,

```
v̂_d / v_d = (r/K) · ‖P_S a_d‖² / ‖a_d‖²,     ‖P_S a‖²/‖a‖² ~ Beta(K/2, (r−K)/2)
```

Checked against the **shipped `haar_seeds` generator**, not a re-implementation,
at 200,000 draws with r = 7:

| K | empirical mean | Beta mean | empirical var | Beta var |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.14293 | 0.14286 | 0.02726 | 0.02721 |
| 2 | 0.28632 | 0.28571 | 0.04542 | 0.04535 |
| 3 | 0.42847 | 0.42857 | 0.05455 | 0.05442 |
| 4 | 0.57151 | 0.57143 | 0.05437 | 0.05442 |
| 5 | 0.71530 | 0.71429 | 0.04533 | 0.04535 |
| 6 | 0.85693 | 0.85714 | 0.02731 | 0.02721 |

Exact to 4–5 decimals in both moments. The law holds.

## 2. It predicts the measured recall, with no free parameters

Take the **exact** per-structure coordinate variances from the caches, inject
noise drawn from the law, recompute top-5% max-component recall. Two variants
isolate whether the shared random subspace matters: `shared` draws one Haar frame
per structure applied to all D coordinates (what the estimator does); `indep`
gives every coordinate its own draw (the purely marginal model).

| system | D | K | measured | predicted (shared) | predicted (indep) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3BPA disjoint | 81 | 3 | 0.484 | 0.522 | 0.510 |
| 3BPA disjoint | 81 | 4 | 0.564 | 0.600 | 0.574 |
| 3BPA disjoint | 81 | 6 | 0.793 | 0.788 | 0.808 |
| 3BPA overlapping | 81 | 4 | 0.693 | 0.757 | 0.770 |
| 3BPA same | 81 | 4 | 0.632 | 0.656 | 0.622 |
| rMD17 ethanol | 27 | 3 | 0.372 | 0.382 | 0.402 |
| rMD17 ethanol | 27 | 4 | 0.486 | 0.472 | 0.482 |
| rMD17 ethanol | 27 | 6 | 0.738 | 0.744 | 0.714 |
| rMD17 aspirin | 63 | 4 | 0.500 | 0.544 | 0.556 |
| rMD17 azobenzene | 72 | 4 | 0.504 | 0.548 | 0.550 |
| rMD17 azobenzene | 72 | 6 | 0.760 | 0.774 | 0.788 |

**The prediction tracks the measurement across six systems, three budgets and a
3× range in D**, with a mean absolute error of ~0.03–0.05 and near-exact agreement
at K=6 (0.793 vs 0.788, 0.772 vs 0.784, 0.738 vs 0.744, 0.834 vs 0.843). The only
inputs are the exact variances and a one-line distributional law.

Two conclusions follow, and the second is the one that corrects the paper.

**The mechanism is estimator variance propagated through a maximum, not bias.**
The marginal finite-K bias is already removed by `std_correction`; what remains
and what destroys recall is the spread of `v̂_d/v_d`, which at K=4, r=7 has
relative sd `sqrt(2(r−K)/(K(r+2))) = 0.408` on the variance and ≈0.204 on σ.
Taking a maximum over D such noisy estimates reshuffles the top of the ranking.

~~The prediction is systematically *optimistic* by roughly +0.03 at K=4… Real
head-space geometry is therefore slightly harder than the isotropic null…~~
**Also withdrawn: the optimism was an artifact of the isotropic assumption, not a
property of the real geometry. Under the corrected model the signed bias is
-0.005.**

## 3. The budget arithmetic: unreachable, not merely unreached

A Chernoff bound on the Gaussian sketch gives `K ≥ 8·log(2D/δ)/ε²` for uniform
componentwise relative accuracy ε at confidence 1−δ. At δ = 0.05:

| system | D = 3N | K for ε=0.5 | K for ε=0.2 | K for ε=0.1 | available |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rMD17 ethanol | 27 | 224 | 1,397 | 5,588 | **7** |
| rMD17 aspirin | 63 | 251 | 1,567 | 6,266 | **7** |
| rMD17 azobenzene | 72 | 255 | 1,594 | 6,373 | **7** |
| 3BPA | 81 | 259 | 1,617 | 6,467 | **7** |
| water (J5) | 576 | 322 | 2,009 | 8,036 | **7** |

Even tolerating a **50%** componentwise error, the requirement exceeds the entire
centred head space by a factor of ~35; at 10% it exceeds it by ~900×. The budget
ceiling is `K ≤ r = M−1 = 7` and is set by the committee size, not by the compute
budget.

So the negative result upgrades from *"we measured low recall at the budgets we
tried"* to *"uniform componentwise accuracy is unreachable by two to three orders
of magnitude at any budget this architecture can afford, and the resulting
estimator variance predicts the observed recall."* That is a much stronger and
more transferable statement, and it costs 14 s of CPU.

## 4. Why this matters beyond ForceSketch

The mechanism is not specific to head-space sketching. **Any stochastic
uncertainty estimate feeding a max-based acquisition rule inherits this
problem**: the maximum of D noisy estimates is dominated by estimator variance
long before the underlying signal is resolved, and a marginal bias correction
does not fix it because the damage is to ranks, not to scale. MC-dropout,
subsampled ensembles and sketched last-layer Laplace approximations all sit in
this class. This is the most transferable sentence the project can write, and it
is now backed by a verified law, a parameter-free prediction, and an explicit
budget bound.

It also explains **why the global statistic wins** (J2a): summing 3N coordinates
concentrates the estimator error instead of amplifying it, so the same budget
buys a far better-behaved score — and J2a independently showed that score is also
the better predictor of physical error.

## Caveats

- ~~The `shared` simulation models coordinate directions as isotropic… its success
  means the real geometry adds little.~~ **Withdrawn — that was the vacuous
  comparison. The real geometry adds a great deal: MAE 0.015 with real
  directions versus 0.033 isotropic. See the Correction.**
- The Chernoff constant (8) is not tight. The conclusion is insensitive to it:
  even a 10× improvement leaves the requirement two orders of magnitude above 7.
- Predictions use 10 noise replicates per structure; the residual +0.03 optimism
  is larger than that Monte-Carlo error and is a real modelling gap.

# Correction: the coupling test was vacuous, and fixing it improved the result

An adversarial review of this analysis found that the two noise models compared
above — `shared` and `indep` — were **identical by construction**, so their
agreement established nothing.

`beta_noise`'s `shared` branch drew one Haar K-frame per structure but applied it
to coordinate directions `a_d` drawn **independently and isotropically**. For a
fixed subspace, independent isotropic directions give independent Beta draws. I
measured it directly: mean across-coordinate correlation within a structure is
**0.0126 for `shared` against 0.0121 for `indep`** — zero coupling either way. The
shared subspace can only induce coupling if the `a_d` are themselves correlated,
which they are in the real data and were not in the simulation.

So the conclusion "coordinate coupling is irrelevant, the failure is fully
marginal" was **unsupported** and is withdrawn.

## The corrected test, using the real head-space geometry

`beta_noise` now has three modes. `shared_real` applies one Haar frame per
structure to the **actual** directions `a_d = Qᵀ F(x)ᵀ e_d` taken from the cache —
what the deployed estimator does. Prediction error against measured top-5% recall,
over 6 systems × 3 budgets:

| noise model | MAE | mean signed bias | max abs error |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`shared_real`** (real geometry) | **0.0154** | **-0.0050** | 0.0540 |
| `shared_iso` (isotropic directions) | 0.0327 | +0.0327 | 0.0680 |
| `indep` (marginal Beta) | 0.0287 | +0.0259 | 0.0840 |

Two things change, both in the paper's favour:

**Coordinate coupling matters.** Using the real geometry roughly **halves** the
prediction error, so the head-space structure is not a negligible detail — the
opposite of what the original text claimed.

**The prediction is now essentially unbiased.** The systematic optimism reported
above (+0.03, growing with D) was an artifact of the isotropic assumption, not a
residual modelling gap. With real directions the signed bias is -0.005 — within
Monte-Carlo error of zero.

So the headline of this section is *stronger* than first written: **the Beta law
plus the exact per-structure variances plus the real head-space directions
predicts the measured top-5% recall to a mean absolute error of 0.015, with no
free parameters, across six systems and three budgets.** The earlier claim of
0.03–0.05 with growing optimism described a deliberately impoverished null model.

The D = 576 water prediction in `J5_WATER.md` §5 was scored under the old
isotropic model and its "growing optimism with D" conclusion is likewise an
artifact; it should be rescored under `shared_real`.

*Everything in Steps 1 and 3 above is unaffected: the Beta law verification and
the Chernoff budget arithmetic do not use `beta_noise`.*

## Reproducibility repair (found by `tools/reproduce.py`)

The offline reproduction tier regenerated this experiment from the git-tracked
caches and got MAE 0.011 against the committed 0.017. Neither was wrong
arithmetically: the script created **one** `torch.Generator` and consumed it
across every (system, K, mode, replicate), so the noise draws depended on how
many systems were in the run and in what order. A record produced that way cannot
be regenerated independently, and the numbers above quietly depended on the
contents of a directory.

The noise draw is now keyed to the item it belongs to — `blake2b(seed, system, K,
mode, replicate)` — so any subset of systems reproduces bit-identically, and
`--seed` is explicit. The table above is the regenerated one. **The conclusion is
unchanged and marginally stronger**: `shared_real` at MAE 0.0154 against
0.0287 (`indep`) and 0.0327 (`shared_iso`), so real geometry still
roughly halves the prediction error.
