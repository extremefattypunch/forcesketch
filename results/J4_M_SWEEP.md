# J4 (reduced) — does any of this need M = 8?

**Exploratory, not pre-registered.** No prediction was registered before running
this and none is scored. It is a descriptive sweep, and saying so is cheaper than
dressing it up as confirmation.

Generated by `experiments/j4_m_sweep.py` from
`results/records/j4_m_sweep.jsonl` (161 rows). Every subset of every size
m = 2…8 is evaluated exactly — 247 subcommittees per system, 20 systems, both
constructions — so the M-dependence is measured rather than extrapolated. Runtime
is a few CPU-minutes; the expensive J4 (retrain at M ∈ {4, 16} × 3 seeds) is
replaced by this, as the plan proposed.

Positives are fixed by the **full** committee's reference error, so the question
is whether a smaller committee still finds the same bad structures — not whether
it gets to redefine which structures are bad.

---

## 1. M-dependence is mild in the mean and severe in the tail

Factor A (AUROC, exact global statistic, top-5% `e_max`) retained relative to
m = 8, averaged over the 20 systems:

| m | retained (mean) | retained (worst system) | **worst single subcommittee** | median overconfidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 90.5% | 82.4% | **54.8%** | 8.4× |
| 3 | 95.0% | 88.7% | 66.0% | 7.1× |
| 4 | 97.0% | 93.0% | **73.2%** | 6.7× |
| 5 | 98.2% | 95.8% | 81.4% | 6.5× |
| 6 | 99.0% | 97.6% | 87.3% | 6.3× |
| 7 | 99.6% | 99.0% | 94.7% | 6.2× |
| 8 | 100% | 100% | 100% | 6.2× |

**Halving the committee costs about 3% of Factor A on average.** M = 8 is
comfortably more than ranking needs, and a practitioner who can only afford four
heads loses very little *in expectation*.

**But the expectation is not what they get.** At m = 4 the best and worst of the
70 possible subcommittees span 0.608–0.886 on azobenzene and 0.482–0.704 on
water-overlapping MD; the worst single 4-member committee anywhere retains only
73% of the full committee's AUROC, and at m = 2 that falls to 55%. So the
defensible statement is: *committee size buys you insurance, not accuracy.* Going
from 4 to 8 heads moves the mean by 3 points and the floor by 27.

This also reframes the workshop's M = 8 choice. It was not needed for the mean
result; it was needed so that the result did not depend on which eight heads got
trained.

## 2. A committee does not become calibrated by getting bigger

Median overconfidence (force RMSE ÷ median per-atom disagreement) goes 8.4× at
m = 2 to 6.2× at m = 8 — a 26% improvement for 4× the compute, and still 6×
overconfident at the end. Magnitude miscalibration is a property of the
construction, not of the sample size, which is consistent with J6's finding that
switching to independently trained models moves the same number from ~24× to
~2.6×. **If you want calibrated magnitudes, change the construction, not M.**

## 3. The global-statistic recommendation is M-robust

Fraction of subcommittees on which the global statistic beats max-component:
median **94.6% at m = 2**, 99.3% at m = 4, 100% at m = 8. The recommendation does
not depend on having a large committee.

The exception is the one already on record: **water PIMD**, where max-component
wins (0% of subsets favour global at m = 4 and m = 8). J5 already scoped the
global recommendation to "molecule-supported, water-neutral"; this sweep sharpens
that to *water PIMD actively prefers max-component*, at every committee size. It
is one system-regime out of twenty, and it is the same regime J6 showed to be
dominated by a single model-failure mode — but it should be named in the paper
rather than absorbed into an average.

---

## What this does and does not settle

- **Settles**: M = 8 is not load-bearing for any decision-quality claim here. The
  Stage 1 limitation "every result is M = 8" is answered downward — results at
  m = 4…7 are materially the same.
- **Does not settle**: what M = 16 would give. Subsetting cannot extrapolate above
  the committee you have. The analytic `(r−K)/(r+2)` variance ratio predicts the
  sketching side improves with r = M−1, but Factor A's saturation by m ≈ 6 makes a
  large gain from M = 16 unlikely. That is a prediction, not a measurement, and it
  is the one place a referee could reasonably ask for the expensive experiment —
  roughly 2 GPU-days for one M = 16 committee.
- **Does not settle** anything about wall-clock: this is decision quality only.
